# CS222 Proposal Grading Rubric (50 points)
SOURCE: CS222 course rubric
TAGS: rubric, evaluation, format

## Format and submission (5 pts)
The proposal adheres to all submission guidelines: page limits, font, file type, and all required sections present. Missing or malformed structure costs points regardless of content quality.

## Figure or visual explanation (7 pts)
The proposal includes at least one clear figure that illustrates the approach — for example, a workflow diagram showing phases, inputs, and outputs, or a conceptual model. A proposal with no visual explanation loses these points. The figure must add understanding, not decoration.

## Motivation and problem framing (6 pts)
The proposal names a concrete problem and stakeholder, establishes why it matters with evidence, and makes the cost of the unsolved problem clear. Abstract or trend-based motivation scores low.

## Novelty and relation to prior work (10 pts)
This is the highest-weighted criterion. The proposal identifies a specific, defensible gap, cites the closest prior work, and explains precisely what remains open. It must show the gap is genuinely unaddressed — not merely assert novelty. Engagement with related work and honest positioning against it is required.

## Method and workflow detail (9 pts)
The proposed approach is specific and reproducible: inputs, steps, techniques, and outputs are stated, and each design choice is tied to the gap. Hand-waving or under-specified methods score low.

## Evaluation plan (7 pts)
The proposal states metrics, baselines, dataset/participants, and a falsifiable success criterion. "We will evaluate it" with no specifics scores low.

## Feasibility, milestones, risks (4 pts)
A realistic timeline with milestones, the main risks, and mitigations. For human-subjects work, ethics/IRB is addressed.

## Writing coherence and polish (2 pts)
The proposal is clearly written, internally consistent, and free of distracting errors.
